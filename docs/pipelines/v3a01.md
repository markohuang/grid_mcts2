## Hypothesis

H: With the pipeline infrastructure validated on fixed map 2 (v2f01), does warm-start self-play + synchronous training produce meaningful gains on **random 5×5 boards** where every game sees a fresh map? Prior work (Round 05, Round 07) suggested generalist training was hard; this is a direct test of whether the pipeline's data-cycling overcomes that.

Under the hood: AlphaZero-style single-player MCTS (no learned dynamics model), pb_c_base=19652 / pb_c_init=1.25, layer-level MDP, plan_cost dense reward, T=1.0 constant sampling at selfplay time, argmax at eval time.

Two trends:
- **Selfplay gets faster as policy concentrates:** c1 664s/game → c5 537s/game (−19%). MCTS explores fewer nodes with a sharper prior, so games terminate sooner on average.
- **Train gets slower as sliding window grows:** 15m (c1, 1 dataset loaded) → 35m (c5, 5 datasets loaded = 2.4M transitions). Buffer load dominates at 5 cycles; training itself is cheap on A100.

Net: cycle cadence is roughly flat at ~3.5h, so the 5-cycle budget behaves predictably.

## Red flag — entropy collapse

Policy entropy dropped from 1.278 (bootstrap) → **0.240** (cycle 5). For reference, uniform over 25 actions = log(25) = 3.22, so cycle_5 is at ~7.5% of max entropy.

Even with aggressive concentration, avg cost is still improving — the policy hasn't gotten *stuck*, it's just very peaked. But this warrants close monitoring in longer runs:

- At current rate (~-40% per cycle across cycles 3-5), entropy could reach <0.1 within a few more cycles → real mode-collapse risk
- Mitigations available: `policy_target_temperature > 1.0`, explicit entropy bonus, larger Dirichlet alpha at root, more diverse maps

## Deterministic eval on held-out validation maps

**Status:** complete (job 59790830). 30 held-out validation maps, `seed_base=100000`, `map_num=2`. Results in `eval/cycle_05b/eval_summary.json`.

Purpose: measure inference quality and compute tradeoff — how few MCTS sims are needed to beat the Kohei greedy baseline with the trained prior?

### Results vs Kohei greedy baseline

Kohei baseline (same 30 maps, no MCTS, ~12ms/map): avg=15.73, min=13, p10=14, p50=16, p90=18.

| method | avg cost | min | p10 | p50 | p90 | avg plan time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kohei greedy** | 15.73 | 13 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 12ms |
| **MCTS sims=200** | 13.63 | 10 | 11 | 13 | 17 | 9.5s |
| **MCTS sims=800** | 12.20 | 10 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 35s |
| **MCTS sims=3200** | **11.53** | **10** | **10** | **11** | **14** | 129s |
| *(selfplay avg, stochastic)* | *12.99* | *8* | *11* | *13* | *15* | *537s* |

**Key findings:**

- **200 sims already beats Kohei** by −2.1 avg cost (13.63 vs 15.73) in 9.5s/map. The trained prior alone is worth more than the greedy baseline.
- **800 sims** cuts avg to 12.20 in 35s — beating the selfplay distribution average (12.99) with 12× fewer sims than training used.
- **3200 sims** reaches 11.53 avg, p50=11, p90=14. The distribution tightens substantially — max cost drops from 18 (Kohei) to 14.
- **Compute/quality tradeoff is steep early, flattening:** 200→800 sims saves −1.4 avg cost for 4× more compute; 800→3200 saves −0.7 for 4× more compute.
- The optimal deployment point is likely **800 sims** (35s/map): beats Kohei by −3.5, beats selfplay avg, and is 4× cheaper than 3200.

## Takeaways

1. **Pipeline automation works at scale.** 11 sequential SLURM jobs, 21h wall clock, zero operator intervention, no failures. Infrastructure validated.
2. **Warm-start + synchronous iteration beats one-shot training** on random boards. Monotonic improvement across 5 cycles confirms the design.
3. **Policy entropy control is the next-priority knob** for longer pipelines. Without it, we risk stagnating at a peaked but suboptimal mode.
4. **Diminishing-returns curve is not yet a plateau** — cycle 4→5 still delivered −0.26 avg cost. Running additional cycles is cheap (linear wall clock) and almost certainly beneficial for another 1-2 points.
